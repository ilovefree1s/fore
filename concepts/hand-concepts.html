FORE hand concepts

Six ways to hold and look at your cards on a phone. Every one keeps the same card face. The question is just how the hand lives on screen: how you flip through it, how you see what you've got, and how you play one. Tap targets are drawn at real size.

1. Arena fan MTG Arena style: hand fanned along the bottom, one card raised

The hole card sits up top, smaller. Your hand is a fan of real cards along the bottom edge, overlapping like you're holding them. Tap one and it lifts out of the fan and grows to full size in the middle with Play under it. Drag up to play, like Arena. The fan compresses as the hand grows.

2. Swipe deck + mini fan full-size cards you swipe through, a fan of minis as the index

Every card is full size, one at a time, and you swipe sideways through them like photos: hole card first, then your hand in order. Under it, a fan of mini cards shows the whole hand at a glance and the one in view lifts out. Tap a mini to jump. Drawing slides the new card in from the right. This one is already built and ready to turn on.

3. Cover flow center card big, neighbors tilted back on each side

Same swipe as the deck, but the cards on either side stay visible, turned away in 3D like an old iPod cover flow. You always see what's next to the card you're on, so a five-card hand reads as a row without a separate index. Swipe or tap a neighbor to bring it forward.

4. Spread sheet tap Hand to lay every card out in a grid

The main screen only ever shows the hole card. A Hand button with a count opens a sheet with all your Power Ups laid out two across, full faces, readable. Tap one to zoom it and play. Boring in the best way: nothing moves, nothing overlaps, and it works the same with two cards or ten.

5. Peek stack cards stacked in your hand, pull the top one up

Your hand is a neat stack at the bottom with just the top edges peeking out, titles readable, like cards tucked in a pocket. Tap any edge and that card slides up to full size over the hole card. Swipe it back down to tuck it away. Compact, and the hole card is always visible behind.

6. Table view hole card up top, hand as a scrolling row of real cards

Like looking down at a table. The hole card sits at the top at half size, and your hand is a row of medium cards you scroll sideways, each one readable on its own without zooming. Tap a card to bring it up full size with Play. The simplest to understand, and every card is always visible at a glance.
